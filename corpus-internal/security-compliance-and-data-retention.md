ISO 27001, penetration test, security questionnaire, VPAT, HECVAT, PCI compliance,
SAQ AOC, accessibility statement, procurement review, data retention, how long do you
keep our data, audit log, GDPR.

Distilled from support replies in the year to July 2026. These arrive from procurement
and IT teams rather than organisers, and a straight answer saves a long thread.

**Certification.** Oxford Abstracts does **not** hold ISO 27001 certification and does
not have a formal third-party penetration test report. What it does have is a
documented Information Security Policy covering encryption at rest and in transit,
multi-factor authentication on the AWS infrastructure, role-based access control that
isolates each client's data, access and audit logging with regular credential
rotation, and Cloudflare and AWS WAF protection. Say this plainly — the absence of ISO
27001 is not a secret and a vague answer invites more questions.

**Documents available on request:** a VPAT, a HECVAT, a PCI compliance certificate and
an SAQ AOC. Ask support.

**Contract renewals.** Where the Standard Terms and Conditions have not changed since
an agreement was executed, the existing agreement remains valid at renewal — no new
signature or re-execution is needed.

**Retention.** Events stay accessible for at least a year after the event has
finished, and packages cover a single event. **Registration data is not retained for
past events, and deleted data cannot be recovered**, whereas submission data from past
events does remain. Anyone who needs registration records long-term should export them
while the event is live.

**There is no audit log.** Oxford Abstracts does not record which admin changed event
details, and there is no change tracker showing what a submitter altered on a
submission, so there is no built-in proof of what an abstract originally said.
Organisers who need that should download the submissions table on a schedule and keep
the files.

**ISBNs** for published abstracts are not something Oxford Abstracts can issue; in the
UK those come from Nielsen.
