Certificate of participation, attendance certificate, my abstract was rejected, when
will I hear the decision, change my presentation date, refund my registration fee,
programme timetable, accommodation, visa letter, I want to submit my abstract, my
paper, acceptance letter, conference dates — asked of Oxford Abstracts directly by an
attendee, presenter or submitter.

Distilled from 377 support tickets in the year to July 2026 — a quarter of everything
support answers, and by far the largest single pattern in the mailbox.

**What is happening.** Oxford Abstracts makes the software a conference runs on. It
does not run the conference. People who find "Oxford Abstracts" on a confirmation
email often assume it is the organising body, and write asking for things only the
organiser can decide or supply: certificates, decisions, refunds, deadlines,
schedules, or the acceptance of an abstract sent as an email attachment.

**The answer.** Say plainly, and early, that Oxford Abstracts provides the software
and is not involved in running the event, so the question needs the event organiser.
Then help them find the organiser rather than leaving them at a dead end:

- The confirmation or notification email they received carries the organiser's own
  reply-to address — that is the right place to write.
- The conference website or programme page usually lists a contact for the committee
  or secretariat.
- If they are logged in, the event's own pages carry the organiser's contact details.

**Do not** promise to forward the message, confirm a decision, issue a certificate,
change a registration or accept an abstract by email. None of that is Oxford
Abstracts' to give, and a hedged answer produces a second ticket.

**Where it is genuinely ours:** signing in, password resets, verification emails not
arriving, and errors in the software itself. Those are worth separating out — someone
who cannot log in to submit is our problem, while what they submit and whether it is
accepted is not.

**Note for the answer layer.** Around one in twelve of these arrive in Spanish,
French or Portuguese. Answer in the language the question was asked in — the
substance is the same.
