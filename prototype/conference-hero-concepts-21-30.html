Why these ten: the first twenty answer “what should the hero look like?”. These answer “what should the hero do?” — and they split along the event lifecycle, which is how an organiser actually thinks. Before the deadline, 24 and 27 sell submission; between deadline and doors, 22, 23, 26 and 30 sell attendance; during, 25 switches the same hero to a live state without a rebuild; and 21, 28 and 29 handle the audiences a single call to action always squeezes out — online delegates, reviewers and sponsors.

Placeholders: every image here is drawn in the page as SVG and every portrait is a silhouette, so the file works offline and nothing depends on a stock library. The motion slot in 25 carries a short generic clip. In production each of these is a slot the organiser fills: a venue photograph, four speaker portraits, a stream or a venue loop.

Concept 25 is the one that changes copy, because that is the point of it: the same hero component reads “register”, “join the live stream” and “watch the recordings” at three moments in the event’s life. Every other concept uses the same words as the first twenty, so they stay comparable.
